	- Testing stability within an epoch:
		- Metrics:
			- *curve correlation*
			- *modulation depth* (used to normalise RMSE)
			- *normalised RMSE*
			- *shift in peak bin*
			- *mean firing rate* (used to calculate mean firing rate)
			- *mean firing rate difference*

		- Procedure:
			- Define a base mask over the positon dataframe
			- Define tuning bins and bin centers to ensure they are 
			  equal across trial subsets.
			- Divide the epoch into the first and second half
			- Compute speed tuning for each trial subset
			- Use the tuning to calculate metrics.
			- Metrics from epoch 2 (Wilbur sess 1):
			```
			Contiguous split summary:
			  median corr: 0.5475027068376564
			  median nrmse: 0.35742026022807766
			  median peak shift (bins): 1.0
			  median mean-rate diff: 0.5125123168925851
			```
			- Now, randomly split the epochs (using a random number generator)
			  50 times, and calculate the metrics for each split.
			- Count "how many times is the null metric less than the contigous
			  , and then take a mean. This is the *p-value*"
			- Now, use the p-value to label units.
			- Currently, correlation and peak shift are used as shape metrics.
			- Mean rate difference and nRMSE are used as rate metrics.
			- For `rate_drift` of `shape_drift` to be true, both metrics' p-value
			  must be less than alpha.
			- A unit is labelled as drifting if either `rate_drift` or `shape_drift`
			  is true.
			Results from 200 runs:
			```
			Within-epoch labels (alpha=0.05):
			  stationary: 201
			  drifting: 58
			  insufficient: 4
			
			Drift type:
			  shape drift: 10
			  rate drift: 53
			
			Null baseline (median across units of per-unit median):
			  corr: 0.49334827077925886
			  nrmse: 0.32066725526641193
			  
			```
	- Testing stability across epochs (first vs last)
		- Calculate tuning for first and last epoch.
		- Calculate the four metrics between these two tunings.
		- For *each unit*: check how many times (mean) the metric is less than
		  the null metric for the same unit.
		- Decide based on p-value and the alpha.
